Arrow-Debreu & FTAP

Notation

X: S×A payoff matrix (rows = states)

p: asset prices

q_s: state price (today's price of 1 unit in state s)

m_s = q_s / π_s: stochastic discount factor

π*_s = R_f · q_s: risk-neutral probability

R_f = 1 / Σ q_s

Arrow-Debreu securities: contingent claims on a commodity. Arrow securities: pay one unit of the numeraire in one state. We mostly use the latter.

Module 1: Asset space vs. state space

Predict: If you buy 1 stock and short 1 bond, where does the portfolio vector land in state space?
But Why? Why flip the axes to states?
In asset space we think in quantities of stock and bond. In state space each point is a payoff pair (Boom, Recession). A stock is just a bundle of state-contingent claims. The tip-to-tail construction is ordinary vector addition: the portfolio payoff is the linear combination w_S · S + w_B · B. This geometry makes completeness and arbitrage visually immediate.
Self-check: What is the cost of the portfolio that pays exactly (1,0)?

Module 2: Synthetic replication sandbox

But Why? Why can't I buy an AD security on the NYSE?
Markets are incomplete: we do not trade a pure state-contingent claim for every possible future. Breeden-Litzenberger shows that the second derivative of option prices with respect to strike recovers the state-price density, so digital options are approximate real-world substitutes. Short positions are legitimate because they are just negative holdings of existing assets; no new securities are created.

Module 3: The disappearing-probability paradox

But Why? Why did P vanish from the pricing formula?
When prices are fixed by the market, the state prices q are determined by the traded assets. The real-world probabilities π only appear inside the SDF m = q/π. Once q is known, the pricing formula Σ q_s x_s no longer needs π. Risk-neutral probabilities are a pricing device, not a belief.

Module 4: Complete vs. incomplete markets

But Why? Why can't I hedge crash risk with only stock and bond?
Stock and bond span only a 2-dimensional plane inside 3-dimensional state space. The pure crash claim (0,0,1) lies off that plane, so it cannot be replicated. Adding an independent put raises rank(X) to 3 and collapses the set of state prices to a single point.

Module 5: From discrete states to continuous curves

But Why? Why sums become integrals?
As N → ∞ the equal-probability bins become a continuous density. The Radon-Nikodym derivative dQ/dP is exactly the continuous analogue of m_s = q_s / π_s. The state-price density itself is not a probability; it is the product of the probability density and the SDF.

Module 6: The Fundamental Theorem of Asset Pricing

But Why? Why does positivity of q equal absence of free lunches?
A strictly positive state-price vector is a separating hyperplane between the set of free lunches and the positive orthant. If no such hyperplane exists, there is a portfolio with non-positive cost and non-negative payoff in every state (with at least one strict inequality). Uniqueness of that vector is equivalent to rank(X) = S, i.e., completeness.
